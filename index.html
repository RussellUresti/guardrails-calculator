How it's calculated

  • Each simulation draws yearly real returns from lognormal distributions. The same random draws are reused for every search and every scenario, so differences between settings come from the assumptions, not from luck.
  • Historical uses Aswath Damodaran's annual US data, 1928–2025 (S&P 500 with dividends, 10-year Treasuries, 3-month T-bills), deflated by CPI. There are 49 overlapping 50-year windows. Pessimistic and optimistic are the worst and best windows for your overall stock/bond/cash mix; consensus averages the mean, volatility and correlation across all 49. Each window's mean and volatility then drive the simulation, so the order of good and bad years is randomized rather than replayed.
  • Projected uses published 10-year (J.P. Morgan: 10–15 year) nominal forecasts for US large caps and US aggregate bonds, converted to real with a 2.4% inflation assumption. Stocks: Research Affiliates 3.1%, Vanguard 4.5% (midpoint of 4–5%), Verus 5.4%, Schwab 5.9%, J.P. Morgan 6.7%. Bonds: Vanguard 4.3% (midpoint), Research Affiliates 4.7%, Schwab 4.8%, J.P. Morgan 4.8%. Cash: Schwab 3.3%. Pessimistic, consensus and optimistic are the lowest, average and highest. Volatility is set to 16.5% for stocks and 6% for bonds.
  • Global stocks (the default) blend each firm's US and international forecasts at your US weight, with international split 75% developed and 25% emerging. J.P. Morgan publishes a global figure (7.0%) directly; Verus is left out because no international figure was available. Averaging geometric returns this way slightly understates a blended portfolio's compound return. For historical scenarios, each year's S&P 500 real return is reduced by the haircut, since world stocks trailed the US by roughly 1%/yr in long-run data from 1900.
  • Success means spending is funded every year through the plan age. Before the access age, only taxable and cash can pay the bills. The taxable account keeps the cash reserve at the same share each year. Withdrawals are grossed up by the tax rates you enter.
  • Triggers scale all balances together, which approximates a market move. Spending stays constant in real terms inside each simulation, as in the Kitces/Income Lab method. Re-run after any adjustment and at least once a year.
